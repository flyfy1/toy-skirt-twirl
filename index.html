👗 Give It a Twirl: A new skirt on a dress form. Swipe to spin it and watch the hem fly out flat.

A new skirt on a tailor's dress form. Swipe sideways and it spins: the hem swings out, rises into a cone and, with a hard flick, stands out flat like a plate. Then it slows and the folds fall back one by one. Four fabrics that each move differently: chiffon, denim, a pleated mamianqun and tulle. A tiny cloth-physics toy, no sign-up.

How to play

  1. Swipe sideways anywhere, with a finger or the mouse. The dress form turns on its stand like a wheel and keeps the speed you gave it.
  2. Below about one turn a second the skirt only swings. Faster, and the hem lifts; at two turns a second it stands out flat. Swipe again to keep it there.
  3. Hold a finger still on it to brake. The form stops; the skirt keeps going and wraps round it.
  4. Tap a hanger at the top to change the skirt: chiffon, denim, a pleated mamianqun or tulle. Each one flies differently.

FAQ

Why does the skirt rise when it spins?

Every bit of cloth is trying to go in a straight line while the waist pulls it round in a circle, so the cloth swings outward, and the faster it goes the higher it rises. A strip 62 cm long starts to lift at about 0.8 turns a second and is nearly level at 2. The toy simulates the cloth and lets this happen by itself.

What is a mamianqun?

A traditional Chinese pleated skirt (马面裙, 'horse-face skirt'). It is made of flat front and back panels with pressed knife pleats between them. When it spins the pleats fan open into a bell and the flat panels stay flat.

Is there anyone wearing the skirt?

No. It is on a tailor's dress form, a linen torso on an iron stand.

Why did things fly out of the denim skirt?

It has pockets.
